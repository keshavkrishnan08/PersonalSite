Safe Fast Charging Robust to Battery Aging, Sole author
A safety filter that keeps lithium-ion fast charging within temperature and voltage limits as the battery ages, with no optimizer in the loop and no state-of-health estimate. I built The 36-line safety path (a fixed 18-step bisection on a reduced-order electro-thermal cell model), the cell model, executable safety tests, reproduction scripts, the paper, and the response to reviewers. Challenge Lithium plating could not be certified: above about 82% charge, even zero current no longer clears the plating margin. I scoped the guarantee to temperature and voltage, said so in the paper, and reported the cycle-life benefit as not found when the experiments did not show one. I learned Narrowing a claim until I could prove it made the paper stronger. The reviewers’ questions were about limits, and stating them up front answered most of them. Results Accepted at IEEE IECON 2026. Within temperature and voltage limits on all 156 aged-cell draws, and on 100 of 100 after recalibrating to a second chemistry, at a median 56 microseconds per step; standard fast-charging protocols broke the limits in all 40 warm conditions tested. Simulation only, no hardware yet. Python, NumPy, SciPy, OSQP baseline GitHub
The Carbon Effect of Flexible Computing Loads, 1st Author, Project Coordinator
When do AI data centers and crypto miners, loads that can switch off at will, raise a grid’s carbon emissions, and when do they lower them? A four-person ASSIP team at George Mason University under Dr. Jiasun Li. I built The general carbon theory and the optimal contract (every file in role1, with 11 verification suites), and the integration of all four roles into one repository with a single reproduction script. I coordinated the team’s weekly plans. Challenge My theory said carbon can fall; a teammate’s solver said it never did. Reconciling them led to the surplus-only contract. A self-audit also caught a sign error in two theorems, and an adversarial review downgraded two claims I had overstated. I learned Checking every closed form numerically, and inviting reviewers to attack the theorems, caught errors that rereading never would. Results Published in the Journal of Student-Scientists’ Research (2026) and presented at the George Mason student conference. Under run-whenever-it-pays dispatch a large flexible load raises emissions; running it only on renewable power that would otherwise be wasted is carbon-optimal. Python, NumPy, SciPy, closed-form analysis, numerical verification PaperDOICodeScreening analysis

Crop Waste Forecasting, Inventor
Forecasts which corn, soybean, and wheat harvests will go unharvested, county by county, by following price shocks through the U.S. supply chain. I built The whole pipeline: loaders for USDA, NOAA, FRED, and Census data (638,622 county-month rows across 2,130 counties, 2015 to 2025), graph diffusion features in which price drops travel a commodity graph and price rises a geographic one, a small neural network, significance tests, and the patent claims. Challenge Full graph neural networks took over 12 hours on a CPU and did not beat simpler inputs, so I replaced them with precomputed diffusion features and a 13,703-parameter network. I learned The model is rarely the bottleneck. Getting the graph and the labels right moved the result more than any architecture did. Results AUC 0.935 against 0.917 for local features alone (p < 0.001), and filed as a U.S. provisional patent with 26 claims. On a split by time, local features still win (0.910 against 0.892), which is the next problem to solve. Python, PyTorch, scikit-learn, XGBoost, statsmodels, GeoPandas GitHub
