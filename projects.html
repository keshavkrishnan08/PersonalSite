ECHO, Wi-Fi sensing
Finds people with Wi-Fi, no cameras. It turns the radio signals between ordinary Wi-Fi devices into a live 3D picture of where people are, and reports space no signal reaches as unknown instead of empty. I built All of it: a seeded radio channel simulator, a signal pipeline (phase cleanup, Hampel filtering), Bayesian radio tomography and a ridge regression floor model, a Hungarian and Kalman tracker, a Next.js interface with nine scenes, a Python reference engine, a FastAPI service, and a C++20 signal library. Challenge Making the Python port match the TypeScript engine bit for bit meant reproducing V8’s Math.hypot and JavaScript’s rounding in Python. Counting unobserved space as occupied collapsed accuracy to an IoU of 0.023, so unknown space is now reported as unknown. I learned An estimate is only useful with its uncertainty. Marking what the system cannot see made it more trustworthy than guessing everywhere. Results On 1,240 held-out frames the floor model reaches an IoU of 0.41 (precision 0.52, recall 0.66), never marks an empty room as occupied across 318 empty frames, and tells floors apart on stairs by height. All on simulated data so far; the hardware adapters are untested. TypeScript, Next.js, React Three Fiber, Web Workers, Python, FastAPI, C++20 GitHub
FIREWATCH, wildfire tracking
Combines satellite, camera, weather, and terrain data into a forecast of where a wildfire will spread, and turns it into evacuation decisions a person approves. I built Data connectors for satellite fire detections, weather models, terrain, fuels, and roads; a time-versioned DuckDB store; smoke detection and camera self-calibration; a 60-member physics spread ensemble corrected by a particle filter; an evacuation decision layer; two PyTorch models; and the operating board. Challenge On real fires the ensemble came out too confident, so I added a fast-spread tail and leave-one-out calibration. Every query is pinned to a point in time, and a test enforces that a forecast can never see future data. I learned For a responder, calibration is the product: a forecast that knows when it is unsure beats a sharper one that does not. Results On pre-registered replays of five real fires, perimeter accuracy improved on all five (IoU +0.01 to +0.13), evacuation calls scored 0.88 precision and 0.82 recall, and one warning came 186 minutes early. Calibrated coverage reached 0.86 against a 0.90 target, so the uncertainty is still slightly too narrow. Python, PyTorch, OpenCV, DuckDB, FastAPI, GOES-18, HRRR, React, TypeScript, deck.gl, MapLibre Live siteGitHub
CROSSING, pedestrian safety
Watches ordinary street video and predicts which pedestrians a vehicle is about to hit, before anyone steps into the road. I built Detection, a ByteTrack-style tracker with Kalman filters, a world model that maps people onto the road in meters, a recurrent network for crossing intention written from scratch in NumPy and gradient-checked, conflict geometry, a calibrated risk score, and privacy controls (face and plate blurring, role-based access, audit logs). Challenge Boxes that overlap on screen can be meters apart in depth, so distance comes from box height and position. Parked cars jitter, so a vehicle only counts as moving after its displacement is confirmed over several frames. I learned Writing a network from scratch, with its gradients checked by hand, taught me more about training than any framework had. Results In simulation: near-miss AUROC 0.951 against 0.868 for a time-to-collision baseline, calibration error 0.002, and crossing intent flagged 2.8 seconds early on average. These numbers come from the simulator; the real-video demo uses the geometric score. Python, YOLOv3, ONNX Runtime, OpenCV, Kalman filters, NumPy, scikit-learn, FastAPI Live siteGitHub
GridWatch, data centers and the power grid
An interactive map of every data center planned or running in Indiana, set against the power grid, with every figure linked to the public filing it came from. I built A hand-curated dataset with a source for every number, a Python pipeline that fetches, validates links, refreshes federal energy data, and watches cited sources for changes, and a map with a 2020 to 2035 timeline, ZIP code lookup, household bill model, comment letter generator, and CSV export. Challenge Getting the numbers right. Comparing my data with an advocacy group’s tracker showed that most megawatt figures that started in trackers disagreed with the filings, which also caught two county errors of my own. Missing figures stay null, never zero. I learned Provenance is a feature: tying every figure to its filing is what lets anyone trust, and correct, a public dataset. Results Tracks 76 projects across 32 counties: 15,142 MW at full build-out, about 43% of Indiana’s peak demand, mapped against 205 plants and 1,237 transmission segments, with 103 cited sources and 84 tests. TypeScript, Vite, MapLibre GL, D3, Python, Overpass API, Vitest, Playwright Live mapGitHub

Axon, astrology for business
Turns a birth date, time, and place into a natal chart, a numerology life path, and a Chinese zodiac sign, then writes a reading from them. I built An astronomy engine for planet positions, the Ascendant, and houses, historical daylight saving conversion, a 30-day timing scorer, language model readings with a written fallback, accounts with row-level security, payments with idempotent webhooks, and scheduled email. Challenge The common reference frame drifts about 0.36 degrees every 25 years, enough to put a planet in the wrong sign near a boundary, and birth times near daylight saving changes need a two-pass conversion. Results Checked against its own definitions: the Ascendant lands on the horizon within a millionth of a degree across 336 cases. Live with paid plans. Next.js, TypeScript, astronomy-engine, Supabase, Stripe, Resend, language models Live siteGitHub
